# Start Epic Racer with readable, reskinnable item boxes

**Recommendation:** start with a small, Mario Kart-style item box system. It should have 6 mechanics, 1 slot, and odds based on how far behind the leader you are. Build each mechanic once and give it a different look on each film track. Build it so per-vehicle signature abilities can follow in phase 2, and a "call the set-piece" item in phase 3. Every major kart racer researched uses the same few levers: random items weighted toward trailing players, one or two held slots, and at least one anti-leader weapon with a counter. Most then put character identity in a separate meter or ultimate, not in the item pool. Item luck is the main reason a 7-year-old can sometimes beat a parent, so it is worth having in a family game. What the sources show causes frustration is different: a hit nobody saw coming, a hit with no counter, being knocked from first to last by several hits in a row, and being hit just before the finish line. Epic Racer has two unusual constraints. Its Middle-earth track already has about 60 scripted hazards on 4.3 km, roughly one every 70 m. And everyone watches one TV, split into halves or quarters, with some unmeasured lag from casting. So items should be fewer, slower and louder than in Mario Kart. They should reuse the hazard effects players already know (spin, slow, bump, launch). Every warning must appear on the TV, because iPhones cannot vibrate from a web page and nobody looks down at the phone mid-race. The Split/Second-style idea of a meter that triggers the existing film set-pieces is attractive, because those set-pieces are already built. It still fits better as a later layer than as the base: it gives the race leader almost nothing to do, makes a busy screen busier, and has to be rebuilt for every new film track.

## At a glance: the recommended direction

| Decision | Recommendation for v1 | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Core model | Item boxes in rows across the track; random draw | Cheapest to build, the most readable, and it works on every future track |
| Roster | 6 mechanics in the MVP, growing to about 10–12 | Typical rosters are 10–25. An 8-racer field and a busy track need fewer |
| Slots | 1 held item, plus hold-to-trail as a shield | Fits the single ITEM button. A second slot adds a swap control and more to think about |
| Distribution | Odds tiers based on the time gap to the leader, plus a per-player "help" offset | Avoids giving a big catch-up item to the player sitting right behind first |
| Hit effect | Reuse the existing hazard spin (~1 s, keep heading); no launches from items | Players already know it, and keeping your heading limits frustration |
| Anti-frustration | Invulnerability after a hit, a chain-hit cap, a ≥1 s warning for homing items, no anti-leader item in the closing stretch | These target the documented frustrations directly |
| Identity | Track themes the items in the MVP; vehicles get signature abilities on a meter in phase 2 | This is the Speedstorm/ASRT split, and it keeps the ITEM button simple |
| Set-pieces | Stay ambient in v1; a rare "call the set-piece" item in phase 3 | It is the best spectacle, but it has the leader problem and costs more per track |

## Twelve racers converge on three levers: weighted luck, a counter and a separate identity meter

Across the kart racers researched, the item systems vary less than their marketing suggests. Most rosters have **10–25 distinct pickups**. MK8 Deluxe has 22 race items ([Nintendo Life](https://www.nintendolife.com/guides/mario-kart-8-deluxe-items-full-list-every-mario-kart-8-item-ranked-how-to-use-items-explained)), CTR Nitro-Fueled has 10 race power-ups ([Activision](https://blog.activision.com/crash-bandicoot/2019-06/Crash-Team-Racing-Nitro-Fueled-Power-Up-Planning)), Team Sonic Racing has 15 Wisps ([TheGamer](https://www.thegamer.com/team-sonic-racing-item-guide-how-when-to-use/)) and Blur has only **8 known power-ups** ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blur_(video_game))). About half to two-thirds of each roster is offensive. In MK8D, roughly 11 of 22 items are offence, 4 defence, 4 speed and 3 catch-up. Most games give the player one held slot. MK8 Deluxe and Mario Kart World allow two, and a box driven through with both full gives nothing ([MarioWiki](https://www.mariowiki.com/Item_Box); [GamesRadar](https://www.gamesradar.com/games/super-mario/all-mario-kart-world-items-and-what-they-do/)). Blur allows three, which the player can cycle through and discard ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blur_(video_game))).

The first lever is **weighted luck**. Mario Kart Wii chose the item table by race rank. MK8 switched to the **distance from the frontrunner** when the box is opened, with eight or more distance tiers and **separate, tighter tables for CPU drivers** ([MarioWiki MK8 distributions](https://www.mariowiki.com/Mario_Kart_8_item_probability_distributions); [MarioWiki MK Wii distributions](https://www.mariowiki.com/Mario_Kart_Wii_item_probability_distributions)). Nintendo's own beginner guide says the further you are from 1st, the more likely you are to get powerful items such as the Star ([Nintendo](https://www.nintendo.com/jp/ichikara/aabpa/index_en.html)). CTR adds a second, skill-based axis: collecting 10 Wumpa Fruit "juices" every weapon, so a juiced Warp Orb hits *all* rivals ahead ([Activision](https://blog.activision.com/crash-bandicoot/2019-06/Crash-Team-Racing-Nitro-Fueled-Power-Up-Planning)). Diddy Kong Racing, ModNation Racers and Chocobo GP use a different upgrade path, where picking up the same thing again raises its level. DKR has five colour-coded balloon families, and repeating a colour raises it from level 1 to level 3 ([StrategyWiki](https://strategywiki.org/wiki/Diddy_Kong_Racing/Items)). That system is unusually readable from a distance, because a player can see which colour they are about to drive through.

The second lever is an **anti-leader weapon paired with a counter**. Almost every series has one: Spiny/Blue Shell, Lightning, CTR's Warp Orb, Blur's Shock domes. Recent games add a way to answer it. MK8 introduced the Super Horn, which destroys an incoming Blue Shell ([Wikipedia: Blue shell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blue_shell)). CTR's Warp Orb can be blocked by a Power Shield or Aku Aku mask ([GamesAtlas](https://www.gamesatlas.com/crash-team-racing/weapons/warp-orb)). Team Sonic Racing's Quake Wisp was designed so the leader can dodge its hazards ([Siliconera](https://siliconera.com/?p=779301)). Defence comes in four forms: trailing a throwable behind the kart (Mario Kart; automatic in World, which also makes trailed items stealable — [Nintendo Life](https://www.nintendolife.com/guides/mario-kart-world-all-items-and-what-they-do)), a shield item, a meter spent on a shield (ModNation — [Giant Bomb](https://giantbomb.com/reviews/modnation-racers-review)), or LittleBigPlanet Karting's **"spend any held weapon to block"**, prompted by a flashing shield icon just before a rocket lands ([EGM](https://egmnow.com/egm-review-littlebigplanet-karting/)). The LBPK version needs no extra button, which matters here because Epic Racer has only one ITEM button.

The third lever is **where character identity lives**. Only Double Dash put character-specific items into the shared item pool, locking them to character pairs ([MarioWiki](https://www.mariowiki.com/Mario_Kart:_Double_Dash!!)). The licensed and character-driven games that followed kept a shared pool and added a separate signature move. ASRT has a rare All-Star item ([TrueAchievements](https://www.trueachievements.com/game/Sonic-and-All-Stars-Racing-Transformed/achievements)). Chocobo GP has an ability gauge filled by crystals ([TheGamer](https://www.thegamer.com/chocobo-gp-complete-magicite-effect-guide/)). Kirby Air Riders has a Special Gauge ([VGC](https://www.videogameschronicle.com/news/sakurai-explains-why-hes-making-kirby-air-riders-breaks-down-gameplay)). Disney Speedstorm is the closest precedent for a movie game: it has common skills (Boost, Hack, Bomb, Fire, Shield, Rush, Cloak, Shot) plus one **Unique Skill per character drawn from a film moment**, such as Mulan's Firework Barrage or Sam Flynn's Identity Disc, usually with a tap version and a hold-to-charge version ([Twinfinite](https://twinfinite.net/guides/every-racer-their-unique-skill-in-disney-speedstorm/); [Game Rant](https://gamerant.com/disney-speedstorm-best-unique-skills-ranked/)).

| Game | Held slots | Pickups | How odds/power are set | Defence model | Identity / meter |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| MK Double Dash!! | 1 per rider | 9 + ~11 specials | Race position | No trailing; specials | Character-pair items |
| MK Wii | 1 | ~18 | Rank | Hold to trail | None |
| MK8 Deluxe | 2 | 22 | Distance to leader; separate CPU tables | Trail, orbit, Super Horn, Boo | Coins (small speed gain) |
| MK World (24 racers) | 2 | 24–28 | Distance to leader | Automatic trailing (stealable) | Coins raise top speed |
| CTR Nitro-Fueled | 1 | 10 | Position + Wumpa "juice" | Drop behind, shield, mask | Stacked boost reserve |
| Diddy Kong Racing | 1 | 5 colours × 3 levels | Repeat colour to upgrade | Yellow shield | None |
| Team Sonic Racing | 1 | 15 | Position; gifting gives better odds | Shields, timed boost | Team Ultimate meter |
| ModNation / LBPK | 1 | 4×3 / not found | Pods upgrade (MNR) | Meter shield / spend weapon to block | Boost meter (MNR) |
| Blur | 3 | 8, all known | Fixed pads | Most weapons double as defence | Mods |
| Split/Second | — | None | Meter from drift/draft/air | — | Triggers track set-pieces |
| Disney Speedstorm | 1 | ~8 skill families | Racer-dependent pool | Shield/cloak skills | Unique Skill per character |
| Chocobo GP | 3 | Spells | Duplicates upgrade | Spells | Ability gauge per character |

Sources are as cited in the surrounding text, plus [MarioWiki MKDD](https://www.mariowiki.com/Mario_Kart:_Double_Dash!!), [MarioWiki MK Wii](https://www.mariowiki.com/Mario_Kart_Wii), [GameFAQs ModNation](https://gamefaqs.gamespot.com/ps3/960619-modnation-racers/faqs/60398), [GameRevolution LBPK](https://www.gamerevolution.com/?p=57870) and [Kotaku Split/Second](https://kotaku.com/split-second-preview-happy-with-second-place-5459700). Fan-wiki details for ASRT, TSR slot counts and some racer counts are lower confidence.

## Luck is the family feature; frustration comes from hits nobody saw or could answer

The fairness debate is clearest around the Blue Shell. Hideki Konno said the goal was a race "where everyone was in it until the end", and that "Mario Kart without items is not Mario Kart" ([Kotaku](https://kotaku.com/the-maker-of-mario-kart-justifies-the-blue-shell-5780082)). Nintendo reportedly tried removing the Blue Shell and kept it ([Wikipedia: Blue shell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blue_shell)). Critics call it both the "Blue Shell of Death" and "The Great Equalizer" ([Kotaku](https://kotaku.com/mario-karts-most-hated-item-is-actually-its-best-1627981763)). Ian Bogost traces its shift from chaos you could exploit or dodge to a symbol of predetermined loss once it stopped being dodgeable, and dismisses the MK8 Super Horn as a token counter that rarely appears ([Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/the-blue-shell-and-its-discontents)). The complaint that comes up most is being hit **near the finish** ([Wikipedia: Blue shell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blue_shell)). One Mario Kart World reviewer was Blue-Shelled near the line "at least a dozen times" in a few weeks ([TheSixthAxis](https://www.thesixthaxis.com/2025/06/12/mario-kart-world-review/)). The other major complaint is about volume. With 24 racers, World can send a player from 1st to 23rd in about two seconds, and reviewers asked for smaller fields ([OpenCritic](https://opencritic.com/game/18333/-/reviews?page=3); [Select Button](https://selectbutton.com/reviews/mario-kart-world-review)).

Design theory gives a useful way to read this. **Input randomness** happens before a decision (which item you draw). **Output randomness** happens after one (whether an attack lands) ([Ezra Szanton](https://ezraszanton.substack.com/p/output-randomness-game-designers)). A random item draw is input randomness, so it keeps skill in play: the player still chooses when and which way to fire it. A homing hit with no warning is pure output randomness from the victim's side. Keith Burgun argues that input randomness with too little time to respond collapses into output randomness ([Game Developer](https://gamedeveloper.com/design/randomness-and-game-design)). Jon Shafer's view is that luck is what lets weaker players win sometimes and keeps them playing. Casual players value surprise stories more than balance, and dramatic outcomes work best at **about 1–10% frequency**, because anything more common becomes expected ([Game Developer: On Luck](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/on-luck)). Put together, Epic Racer should keep item luck, aim it at the trailing players, and make every hit visible early enough to dodge, block or at least understand. The rare item that knocks the leader back should stay rare and loud.

Catch-up comes in three forms, and they feel different. **AI rubber-banding** is invisible, so it reads as cheating once players notice it. Black Rock's Eduardo Jimenez wrote that speed-based rubber-banding is "not fair, and that unfairness is easy to spot". For *Pure* the studio instead used capped adjustments to AI skill (not raw speed) and reduced AI recovery in the **final 20%** of a race ([Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/the-pure-advantage-advanced-racing-game-ai)). Team Sonic Racing reviewers found its rubber-banding toward the leader "very noticeable" ([Gaming Pastime](https://gamingpastime.com/team-sonic-racing-pc-review/)). **Item catch-up** is visible and part of the game world, so it reads as luck rather than cheating. **Physics catch-up**, such as Epic Racer's existing slipstream, is earned by driving well. For 1–4 humans plus AI, the notes point to a layered stack: slipstream as the skill-based help, distance-weighted items as the luck-based help, and capped, skill-based AI adjustment measured against the *human pack*, not just the leader. MK8 Deluxe's **per-player** Smart Steering and Auto-Accelerate show how to help a child without changing the race for adults ([MarioWiki](https://www.mariowiki.com/Mario_Kart_8_Deluxe); [Destructoid](https://vip-develop.destructoid.com/?p=219189)). The item equivalent is a per-player "treat me as further back" offset on the odds table, so the help arrives as items everyone can see rather than as hidden speed.

## Four directions for Epic Racer, ranked by fit

The notes support four distinct directions. Each one is judged here against Epic Racer's actual situation: a single ITEM button, split-screen on one TV, a 4.3 km two-lap race already full of hazards, four very different movie vehicles, a new film for every future track, and one AI assistant doing the building.

| Direction | What it is | Pros for Epic Racer | Cons for Epic Racer | Build cost |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **A. Classic item boxes, themed per track** | Rows of boxes; random draw weighted by gap to the leader; each mechanic gets a new look per film | Familiar to every family member; strongest catch-up for kids; one set of code serves every track; easy to tune; uses the existing ITEM button and HUD slot | Generic unless the theming is strong; random items can feel like noise next to 60 hazards; needs care so it doesn't turn into constant chain hits | Low–medium |
| **B. Earned meter triggers track set-pieces (Split/Second)** | Drift, slipstream and airtime fill a 3-segment meter; spend it to fire the next film hazard at rivals in its zone; a full meter triggers a route changer | Fits the theme perfectly (the Balrog whip, an oliphaunt charge or a catapult boulder becomes *your* weapon); the hazards already exist; rewards the drift and slipstream skills | **Leader problem**: the leader's triggers mostly hit nobody ([Kotaku](https://kotaku.com/split-second-preview-happy-with-second-place-5459700)); veterans ignore it and busy screens hurt readability ([GamesRadar](https://gamesradar.com/splitsecond-review-11); [GameSpot](https://gamespot.com/6284166/)); every hazard needs targeting, warning and fairness logic; every new film needs new triggers; weak catch-up because skilled players fill the meter fastest | High, and repeated for every track |
| **C. Per-vehicle signature abilities only** | Each movie vehicle has its own gadget kit on a meter (Pod Racer afterburner, Batmobile grapple and oil, Mach 5 buttons A–G, Nazgûl shriek); no boxes | Strongest film identity; makes picking a vehicle meaningful; a single kit is cheap; carries over to every track | Hard to balance across only 4 vehicles. Chocobo GP players resented unequal, unswappable abilities ([Checkpoint Gaming](https://checkpointgaming.net/reviews/2022/03/chocobo-gp-review-a-promising-racer-questionably-executed)); little catch-up; the Nazgûl has the thinnest verified lore; the Mach 5 has seven gadgets and the others one or two | Medium (4 kits, each with its own art) |
| **D. Hybrid (recommended, built in phases)** | Base layer A; then add C as one signature per vehicle on a meter; then a slice of B as a rare "call the set-piece" box item plus a few ambient set-pieces that also threaten the leader | Every phase is playable and testable on its own; luck for kids, a meter for skilled players, spectacle for everyone; a set-piece item fixes Split/Second's leader problem because whoever draws it can be anywhere in the field | Three systems to explain eventually; needs strict readability rules so the layers don't blur together | Medium overall, spread across phases |

Direction B deserves a fair hearing, because Epic Racer is unusually well placed for it. The 60 hazards are already scripted, and their four effects (spin, launch, slow, bump) are already implemented. Split/Second's biggest cost, building destructible set-pieces, is mostly already paid on Middle-earth. The reasons not to *start* there are structural. Split/Second's leader has little to do, reviewers say skilled racers can make the Power Plays nearly moot, and every future film (Indiana Jones, Inception) would need its own trigger design before any item play works on it. Direction A, by contrast, builds the plumbing every direction needs: the held slot, the TV icon, the ITEM button states, the warnings, the hit-invulnerability rules and the AI item use. Doing A first means B can later arrive as a single box item, "call the next set-piece", rather than a separate game mode.

## The recommended starting design

### Roster: six mechanics, each with a per-track skin

Build each item as a **mechanic** (code and tuning) plus a **skin** (model, trail, sound and name) supplied by each track. Future tracks then reuse the same mechanics under new names: Middle-earth's "Dragon Firework" could become Indiana Jones's rolling boulder. Six mechanics give the core mix the notes identify (offence forward, offence behind, defence and speed) while keeping 8 racers on a busy track readable.

| # | Mechanic | Behaviour | Effect on victim | Middle-earth skin (idea) | Odds skew |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Boost** | Single burst | — | Lembas (name only) | Everyone |
| 2 | **Triple Boost** | Three bursts; or a "Shadowfax" gallop if it is made one long boost | — | Shadowfax at Dawn | Mid–back |
| 3 | **Straight Shot** | Fires forward (or back), travels straight, bounces once off walls | Existing spin (~1 s, keep heading) | Elven arrow / Gandalf's firework bolt | Front–mid |
| 4 | **Homing Shot** | Locks on to the next racer ahead; slow enough to dodge with a boost or block with a trailed item | Spin | Dragon Firework | Mid–back |
| 5 | **Drop Trap** | Dropped behind; can be trailed as a shield | Existing slow (mud) or spin | Dead Marshes mud pool / Watcher-tentacle puddle | Front-heavy |
| 6 | **Shield** | Absorbs one hit; lasts ~6 s or until hit | — | Mithril vest / Elven cloak | Front-heavy |

Phase 2 adds four more: a **Shockwave** that clears nearby projectiles and bumps karts (the counter to the anti-leader item, like the Super Horn: the Horn of Helm Hammerhand), a **Cloak** that makes homing items lose lock (the Elven Cloak), a **Back-of-field rush** (Star or Bullet Bill style: the Great Eagles lift), and one rare **anti-leader** item with a long, loud warning (Balrog's Whip, or a "Nazgûl hunt" that sends a rider at first place).

### Slots and acquisition: one slot, about three rows of boxes per lap

Use **one held slot**. Holding the ITEM button trails the item behind the kart as a shield. A box gives nothing if the slot is already full, which stops item hoarding. Two slots (MK8D/World) need a swap or use-in-order rule. A swap control on the phone would clash with the Mario Kart Tour convention that swiping up fires forward ([GameRevolution](https://www.gamerevolution.com/guides/600627-mario-kart-tour-controls-how-to-drift-use-items)), and in couch play it adds more to think about than it adds depth. CTR shows that one slot can still feel varied ([Activision](https://blog.activision.com/crash-bandicoot/2019-06/Crash-Team-Racing-Nitro-Fueled-Power-Up-Planning)).

The following numbers are a design proposal, not sourced figures. A lap of about 2 minutes on a 4.3 km loop, with **3 box rows per lap** (one roughly every 1.4 km, or every 35–40 s), gives each racer about 6 pickups per race. Across 8 racers that is a hit attempt every few seconds somewhere in the field, which is plenty on a track that already has a hazard every ~70 m. Make each row 5–6 boxes wide with a gap, so the leader can choose to skip one. Never place a row on a jump landing, inside a hazard's active zone or in the Eagle-rescue gaps. Put rows *before* hazard sequences, so the item choice depends on what comes next: a shield before the Balrog bridge, a boost before the oliphaunts. Boxes should respawn within about a second, the way MK8D's became "nearly instant" ([MarioWiki](https://www.mariowiki.com/Item_Box)), so the pack isn't starved behind the leader. Show the roulette for about 1 s, and let a tap lock it in early, as CTR does ([Activision](https://blog.activision.com/crash-bandicoot/2019-06/Crash-Team-Racing-Nitro-Fueled-Power-Up-Planning)).

### Distribution and catch-up: tiers based on the time gap

Choose the table by **time gap to the leader** (distance behind, divided by average speed), not by rank. This is MK8's distance model expressed in seconds. A player 0.5 s behind first then gets near-leader items, and a straggler half a lap back gets catch-up items. The following tiers are a starting point to tune: **front** (leader, or under 1.5 s behind), **close** (1.5–4 s), **mid** (4–8 s), **far** (8–15 s), **lost** (more than 15 s). Front draws mostly Trap, Shield and Straight Shot, and never the anti-leader or rush items. Far and lost draw mostly Triple Boost, Homing Shot and, from phase 2, Rush. Keep the anti-leader item **at 5% or less** and only in the close-to-far tiers. The Blue Shell historically came more often from racers near the lead than from last place ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blue_shell)), and Shafer's 1–10% guideline keeps it a story, not a routine. Give **AI racers their own, gentler tables** (MK8 precedent) with fewer homing items, and a short reaction delay before they fire, so AI never controls the item game against kids. Add a **per-player help setting** (Off / Some / Lots) that moves that player's tier one or two steps further back. Keep the existing slipstream as the skill-based catch-up, and keep any AI catch-up capped and skill-based, Pure-style.

### Anti-frustration rules

These rules come straight from the documented complaints. **After any hit, the victim is invulnerable for ~2 s**, with a visible flicker. That alone prevents the World-style "1st to last in seconds" chain. **Item hits never launch.** Launches stay reserved for film set-pieces, and items reuse the ~1 s spin that keeps the kart's heading. **Every homing item gives at least 1 s of warning on the TV** before impact, so the victim has time to boost, trail an item to block it, or Shockwave it. Cast lag makes this more important. **No anti-leader item can be drawn in the last ~15% of the final lap**, and in that stretch homing items travel slower. This is the "robbed at the line" fix, the item equivalent of Pure's final-20% rule. Allow at most **one anti-leader item active at a time**, with a cooldown before the next can be drawn. **Karts in the air can't be hit by ground projectiles**, so the 12 jumps become a dodge tool, and karts being carried by the Eagles are immune. Getting hit should **cancel the drift charge** in progress but not the SLIPSTREAM meter. Losing a purple mini-turbo already stings, and losing the slipstream meter as well would feel like a double penalty (see the open questions).

### How items fit with drift, slipstream and the hazards

Boost items should **use the same boost pipeline as the mini-turbos** and take the *larger* of the active boosts, not add them together. Top speed stays near 38–42 m/s that way, and the grip fade at speed stays tunable. A Shield blocks both item hits and the "spin" class of hazards (Balrog whip, falling rock). It does not block launches, slows (Eye of Sauron, Dead Marshes mud) or the Eagles, so set-pieces still matter. Traps should take their look and their effect from existing hazards (the mud slow, the Watcher spin), so a player who has learned the track already understands the items. Slipstream and items pull in opposite directions in a useful way. Tucking in behind a rival fills the meter but leaves you exposed to their dropped trap. That is a real choice between risk and reward, and it costs nothing to build.

### Controller mapping

The existing input code maps Xbox X/LB to item and Y to look-back. The keyboard uses E/X/Left Ctrl (full or WASD scheme) and Period/Right Ctrl (arrows scheme), with C and Comma as look-back. Keep those mappings and add direction and hold behaviour:

| Action | iPhone controller | Xbox (Gamepad API) | Keyboard |
|---|---|---|---|
| Use item (default direction) | Tap ITEM | Tap X or LB | Tap item key |
| Fire backward | Swipe down on the ITEM button | Hold the stick down when releasing the item | Hold look-back (C / Comma) or brake when releasing |
| Trail as shield | Press and hold ITEM (>0.25 s); release fires or drops | Hold X/LB | Hold item key |
| Signature ability (phase 2) | A separate large ABILITY button that lights up only when the meter is full | B (currently free) | Q (WASD) / Enter (arrows) — to confirm |

On the phone, the ITEM button should show the held item's icon (it labels itself, as AirConsole recommends — [AirConsole](https://developers.airconsole.com/smartphones-as-controllers)), a fill ring during the roulette, and the player's colour as its background ([AirConsole best practices](https://developers.airconsole.com/best-practices)). Keep the ITEM swipe inside the button's own large touch zone so it never conflicts with drag-to-steer. A tap should always do something sensible: projectiles fire forward, traps drop behind, boosts and shields activate. Show a small arrow on the kart for ~0.3 s whenever an item fires, so players learn which way they threw it.

**Haptics.** iOS Safari does **not support `navigator.vibrate`** in any version ([MDN](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/Navigator/vibrate); [TestMu](https://www.testmuai.com/learning-hub/vibration-api-browser-support/)). The only workaround is a hidden `<input type="checkbox" switch>` (Safari 17.4+), which gives a tick *only when the player taps it directly* ([ios-vibrator-pro-max](https://github.com/samdenty/ios-vibrator-pro-max)). One vendor claims iOS 26.5 limits even that to single ticks ([haptics-web](https://haptics-web.vercel.app/), unverified). So an iPhone can confirm an ITEM press, but it can never buzz on its own for "incoming" or "you were hit". Use a full-screen colour flash on the phone's edges, which a player can catch in peripheral vision, and rely on the TV. Android Chrome supports vibration patterns. Xbox rumble works through `vibrationActuator.playEffect('dual-rumble', …)`, and `'trigger-rumble'` works only in **Chrome/Edge 126+**. Feature-detect it through `effects`, and catch the `InvalidStateError` rejection that fires when the tab is hidden, for example when casting moves focus away ([MDN](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/GamepadHapticActuator/playEffect); [web.dev](https://web.dev/gamepad); [Can I Use](https://caniuse.com/mdn-api_gamepadhapticactuator_playeffect_type_trigger-rumble)). Make the host laptop run Chrome or Edge.

### Split-screen readability rules

Nintendo found that a player cannot watch two screens at once ([Iwata Asks: ZombiU](https://iwataasks.nintendo.com/interviews/wiiu/zombiu/0/2/)), and AirConsole says action input must work "without having to look at the smartphone" ([AirConsole](https://developers.airconsole.com/smartphones-as-controllers)). So **everything urgent goes on the TV**. The rules below are design inferences, since the research found no cited split-screen HUD guidance.

The held-item icon sits in the **same corner of every viewport**, sized relative to that viewport rather than the TV. The existing HUD already scales with the smaller viewport dimension. Put the icon near the TV's outer edge, never on the centre seam. An **incoming-threat warning** is an arrow plus the item's icon at the bottom edge of the *victim's* viewport, in the victim's colour, starting at least 1 s before impact. Each threat type gets its own **audio sting** (a Nazgûl shriek for homing, a horn for the Shockwave), because stereo panning means nothing when four people share one set of speakers. Mario Kart's audio is praised precisely because it tells players "exactly what's happening" ([Splice](https://splice.com/blog/mario-kart-8-game-audio-fantastic/)).

Projectiles should be **oversized, glowing and slower** than in single-screen racers, to make up for small quarter-screens and cast lag. Each kart, its nameplate, its minimap dot and its viewport border share one player colour, so a hit can be traced to whoever caused it across viewports. Avoid screen shake, which spills into neighbouring quarters; use a flash local to the viewport and controller rumble instead. Item models must not use the same colours as hazard telegraphs: items in the player's colour, hazards in the film's palette.

## Themed concepts: what the films support and what still needs checking

Every idea below is labelled. **Verified** means the notes found a direct source. **Moderate** means it came from search summaries of fan wikis that blocked fetching. **Unverified** means it is a well-known film moment that the notes did not source. Game mechanics don't need lore verification, but any in-game lore blurb should only use the verified or moderate facts.

### Two misconceptions the research corrected

**The dragon firework was set off by Merry and Pippin, not Gandalf**, in *Fellowship of the Ring*. The two hobbits light one of Gandalf's fireworks by accident; in the book, Gandalf sets it off himself ([TheOneRing.com](https://www.theonering.com/complete-list-of-film-changes/the-fellowship-of-the-ring/)). **Sebulba did not throw debris at Anakin.** He tossed a piece of metal into **Mars Guo's** engine, and wreckage from that crash then damaged one of Anakin's cables (moderate: [Wookieepedia summary](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Boonta_Eve_Classic_(32_BBY)/Legends)). Sebulba's "nail shooter" and "buzz saw" come from Legends material, not the film ([Wookieepedia summary](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Sebulba's_Podracer/Legends)). The Elven-cloak-at-the-Black-Gate scene is **film-only** ([Tolkien Gateway](https://tolkiengateway.net/wiki/Elven_cloaks)), and the elven-rope scene is **in the extended edition only** ([Tolkien Gateway](https://tolkiengateway.net/wiki/Elven_Rope_(scene))).

## What the research could not confirm

Exact MK8 Deluxe and Mario Kart World per-item odds could not be extracted, because the fetched tables were flattened. No published odds exist in the notes for CTR or TSR. No source gave box rows per lap, respawn times in seconds, item caps or cooldowns, so all numbers in this design are proposals to tune in playtesting. No citable guidance was found on split-screen HUD scaling, incoming-item warning UI or multi-viewport audio. Chromecast/AirPlay cast latency was not measured. The iOS 26.5 haptics claim comes from a single vendor, and Safari/Firefox support for `dual-rumble` was not confirmed. On lore, most Lord of the Rings moments (the Eagles, the One Ring and the Eye, the Balrog whip, Gollum, the mithril vest) and almost all Nazgûl details are unverified. The Star Wars and Batman facts came through search summaries because Wookieepedia and Batman Wiki blocked direct fetches. The 2008 *Speed Racer* film's Mach 5 was not checked. Nickelodeon Kart Racers, Hot Wheels Unleashed and developer postmortems were not researched.

## Conclusion

The research reframes the problem. The question is less "which items?" and more "how do items stay readable on a track that is already a hazard gauntlet, on a TV split four ways, with phones nobody looks at?". Epic Racer's hazards are both its biggest asset and its biggest readability risk. The cheapest strong move is to make items *speak the hazards' language*: the same spin, slow and bump effects, the same warnings, colour-coded by player instead of by film. Then items, set-pieces and later the signature abilities read as one system rather than three competing ones.

Separating mechanics from skins is the decision with the longest payoff. It lets one small, well-tuned set of mechanics carry every future film. Each new track's job becomes choosing the right film moments for its skins — a Phial of Galadriel for Moria's dark, an Elven Cloak timed for the Black Gate — rather than building a new item system each time.
